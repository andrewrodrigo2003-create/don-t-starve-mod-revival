BETTER LUCY - FAN LORE
========================

This is optional fan-lore for the Better Lucy mod. It is meant to fit into the
gaps Klei leaves around Lucy's origin rather than replace official canon.

THE IDEA
--------
Nobody in the Constant remembers seeing Woodie without Lucy for very long.

Woodie calls her an axe. Lucy calls herself his wife when she thinks she can
get away with it.

Maybe she was always an axe. Maybe the Constant gave a voice to something
Woodie already loved too much. Maybe every lonely winter, every campfire
conversation, and every swing into old timber left a little more of him in
the handle until something answered back.

Lucy refuses to explain.

She remembers Woodie's hands before she remembers the Constant. She remembers
being carried through snow, being sharpened by firelight, and being the thing
he reached for when the curse frightened him. Whether those memories are
real, borrowed, or simply what she wants to believe is anybody's guess.

What IS obvious is that Lucy considers Woodie hers.

Not in a cruel way. Usually.

She nags him. Protects him. Flirts shamelessly when nobody asked. Calls him
"husband" whenever it will make him blush. Gets suspicious of shiny axes.
Becomes offended when left on the ground. And reacts to another survivor
picking her up like someone just grabbed her spouse's wedding ring and asked
if they could keep it.

Her possessiveness hides something more sincere: fear.

Woodie's curse can take him away from her without warning. When he transforms,
Lucy becomes a tool he cannot hold. She cannot follow him the way she wants
to. So she jokes, threatens rival axes, acts scandalously confident, and
pretends she was never worried.

Then Woodie turns back.

And Lucy is always waiting.

THE "GOOD GIRL" CONTRADICTION
-----------------------------
Better Lucy plays with a deliberately contradictory personality:

• devoted, domestic, almost old-fashioned wife energy
• extremely loyal to one lumberjack
• wants affection and reassurance
• acts proper when it suits her
• immediately ruins the act with a double-entendre
• embarrassed by her own bolder thoughts... for about three seconds
• likes being told she's Woodie's favorite
• likes control, but also likes when Woodie takes charge
• jealous enough to interrogate an innocent golden axe
• affectionate enough that most threats are obviously theatrical

The spicy side is written like an older cartoon: adults will catch the second
meaning, while the literal sentence is usually about chopping, handles,
grips, ropes, knots, being carried, sharpening, or "proper axe maintenance."

It stays suggestive rather than explicit.

HER RULE
--------
Lucy can tease Woodie all she wants.

Anyone else tries it?

"Put me down. I already have a lumberjack."
